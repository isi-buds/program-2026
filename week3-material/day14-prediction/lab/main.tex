Unadjusted Model: $E[Y] = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X$

\vspace{2pt}

Adjusted Model: $E[Y] = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1 X + \gamma_2 W$

\vspace{5pt}

Proposition 1: 
$$E[\hat{\beta}_1] = \gamma_1 + r_{XW} \sqrt{\frac{\text{Var}(W)}{\text{Var}(X)}} \gamma_2$$

Proposition 2: 
$$\text{Var}(\hat{\gamma}_1) = \frac{\sigma^2_{Y|X} - \gamma_2^2 \text{Var}(W|X)}{n \text{Var}(X) (1 - r_{WX}^2)}$$

$$\text{Var}(\hat{\beta}_1) = \frac{\sigma^2_{Y|X}}{n \text{Var}(X)}$$




In this lab you will conduct a simulation study to confirm Propositions 1 \& 2 in order to investigate the impact of omitting confounders or important predictors of response on inference regarding some predictor of interest in linear regression. Let $X \sim N(0, \sigma_X^2)$ be the predictor of interest, $W \sim N(0, \sigma_W^2)$ with $\text{corr}(X, W) = \rho$ be the confounder or the nonconfounding important predictor. Note that $W$ will tend to be a confounder when $\rho \neq 0$ and $\gamma_2 \neq 0$, and it will tend to be a nonconfounding predictor of interest (precision variable) when $\rho = 0$ and $\gamma_2 \neq 0$. Let $Y_i \sim N(\mu_i, \sigma^2)$ be the response such that
$$ \mu_i = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1X_i + \gamma_2W_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, n. $$

For each of the cases listed below, we will simulate datasets (at least 1000) and perform linear regressions of $Y$ on $X$ alone and of $Y$ on $X$ and $W$. For each case given below, compare the results from the two regression models with respect to (1) the probability of rejecting $H_0: \gamma_1 = 0$, (2) the bias of $\hat{\gamma}_1$, and (3) the standard errors of $\hat{\gamma}_1$.

Write a simulation study to complete the tables below for the case when we regress $Y$ on $X$ alone and when we regress $Y$ on $X$ and $W$. For your simulation, take $\sigma_X^2 = 1.25$ and $\sigma_W^2 = 2$. Note that the package \texttt{mvtnorm} may be useful if you are writing your simulation in R. Comment on your results in terms of how they agree with Propositions 1 \& 2 and the relevance to choosing adjustment variables in practice.
